Impact framework

Three layers, one story

The reporting architecture separates the evidence types so claims stay proportionate to what the data can actually support.

01

Credible outputs

The scale of activity: food rescued, meals provided, volunteers, shifts and related operational outputs.

02

Stakeholder outcomes

Change over time for the people and volunteers connected to FoodFilled, including retention and wellbeing.

03

Economic value

Conservative estimates that translate rescued food and donated resources into a common dollar frame for funders and donors.

Impact measures

Four questions the dashboard can answer

Each measure combines a clear question, proportionate data collection and a defined reporting target.

Volunteer retention

Quarterly

What percentage of new volunteers are still active at 6 and 12 months — and how does that differ by recruitment channel?

Data

Salesforce start/exit dates and recruitment source from onboarding records; mostly automated.

Burden

Quarterly review with approximately one hour of analysis.

Target

Establish a baseline, then improve 12-month retention year-on-year.

Wellbeing improvement

Quarterly / biannual

How does wellbeing change among people experiencing food insecurity before and after receiving food support?

Data

Short surveys through partner agencies, linked using anonymous participant IDs.

Burden

Approximately 3–5 minutes per survey plus limited analysis time.

Target

Establish a baseline, then demonstrate sustained positive improvement over time.

Food replacement value

Annual

What would the rescued food cost if recipients or agencies had to purchase an equivalent amount?

Data

Calibrated kilograms rescued multiplied by a researched and cited average dollar value per kilogram.

Burden

Approximately half a day of annual analysis.

Target

Publish a conservative “at least $X” estimate with assumptions clearly stated.

Value per $ donated

Annual

How much estimated community value is created for each dollar donated to FoodFilled?

Data

Economic value estimates, financial statements, and explicit attribution and deadweight assumptions.

Burden

Minimal additional burden once the broader economic-value model exists.

Target

Publish a conservative “at least $X of value per $1 donated” estimate.

Several observed variables are entered together. The model estimates the relationship between each factor and the missed-shift outcome while holding the other included factors constant. This helps distinguish overlapping patterns, but the result remains an association unless a stronger causal design is used.

Volunteer retention

Kaplan–Meier analysis · compare how long different cohorts remain active
Group A Group B
Each downward step represents an observed exit. Volunteers who are still active contribute information up to the end of their observed period. A log-rank test compares the overall curves.
Instead of reducing retention to one number, the curve uses the timing of exits. This lets FoodFilled estimate the proportion remaining active at six and twelve months and investigate whether different recruitment cohorts or channels have different retention patterns.

Across a matched sample, the analysis looks at all individual before→after changes. The headline result is the mean change with a 95% confidence interval.

The paired t-test asks whether the average change differs from zero. A Wilcoxon signed-rank test is then used as a robustness check if the bounded wellbeing scores are skewed.
A paired t-test is the headline test because it is straightforward to communicate and works well for the planned sample sizes. A Wilcoxon signed-rank test provides a robustness check if the bounded wellbeing scores are skewed.

Cost & administration

More evidence. Proportionate effort.

The framework deliberately differentiates between measures that can be automated and outcomes that need targeted sampling.

High impact · low burden

Automate

Volunteer retention and economic calculations built from data FoodFilled already records.

High impact · medium burden

Sample

Recipient and volunteer wellbeing through selected follow-up cohorts.

High burden · unclear value

Avoid

Manual monthly surveys, duplicate data entry and metrics without a decision attached.

Low burden · high leverage

Reuse
